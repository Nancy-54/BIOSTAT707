Checkpoint1

1. Table1
The analytic cohort included 4,000 ICU patients. Of these, 3,446
(86.15%) survived hospitalization and 554 (13.85%) died in the hospital.

The mean age of the cohort was 64.2 years (SD 17.6). Patients who died
in the hospital were older on average than those who survived
hospitalization (70.0 vs. 63.3 years). Overall, 43.8% of patients were
female and 56.1% were male. The most common ICU type was the Medical ICU
(37.0%), followed by the Surgical ICU (26.7%), Cardiac Surgery Recovery
Unit (21.9%), and Coronary Care Unit (14.4%). Height was missing for
47.3% of patients in the admission/static Table 1 data, while admission
weight was missing for 8.2%.


2. Outcome Summary
In-hospital death was 13.85% (554/4,000). SAPS-I was available for
3,810 patients, with a mean of 14.96 (SD 5.18) and median of 15 (Q1 11,
Q3 19). SOFA was available for 3,856 patients, with a mean of 6.68 (SD
4.02) and median of 7 (Q1 3, Q3 9). Length of stay was available for
3,940 patients, with a mean of 13.66 days (SD 12.21) and median of 10
days (Q1 6, Q3 17).

For the Survival variable, 2,526 patients (63.15%) had no recorded
survival time (-1), while 1,474 (36.85%) had a recorded survival time.
Among patients with a recorded survival time, the median was 70 days (Q1
10.25, Q3 479), with a range from 0 to 2,600 days.


3. Missingness Summary
Several routinely measured variables had little or no patient-level missingness, including
Age (0.0%), ICUType (0.0%), Gender (0.1%), HR (1.6%), BUN (1.6%),
Creatinine (1.6%), HCT (1.6%), Temp (1.6%), GCS (1.6%), and Platelets
(1.7%). In contrast, some measurements were absent for a large
proportion of patients. TroponinI had the highest missingness (94.9%),
followed by Cholesterol (92.4%), TroponinT (78.4%), RespRate (72.5%),
Albumin (59.6%), ALP (57.8%), Bilirubin (57.0%), ALT (57.0%), AST
(56.9%), and SaO2 (55.2%). Lactate was missing for 45.4% of patients.

These patterns suggest that missingness is plausibly informative rather
than simply random noise. In an ICU setting, whether a test is ordered
can reflect the patient’s clinical condition and clinicians’ assessment
of what measurements are necessary. For example, a patient with no
lactate measurement is not equivalent to a patient whose lactate was
measured and found to be normal. The observed differences in measurement
frequency by in-hospital death status are consistent with an
informative measurement process. However, these descriptive results
alone do not establish a specific missing-data mechanism or prove that
missingness itself causes the outcome.

4. Information Available at Prediction Time

The prediction-time information is restricted to data available during
the first 48 hours of the ICU stay. This includes the admission/general
descriptors (Age, Gender, Height, ICUType, and Weight) and the
time-series clinical variables measured during that 48-hour window. For
repeatedly measured variables, only measurements recorded within the
first 48 hours are available at prediction time. The fact that a
variable was not measured during this window is also observable at
prediction time and can itself be represented as missingness
information.

Information occurring after the first 48 hours must not be used as a
predictor. In particular, the final in-hospital death outcome, total
length of stay, and later survival information are not available at
prediction time and therefore should not be used as predictor variables.
Outcome-related variables are summarized here for cohort
characterization, not treated as prediction-time predictors.
